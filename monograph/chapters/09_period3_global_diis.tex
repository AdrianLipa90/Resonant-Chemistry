\section{Third Period: Global SCF Stabilization from Na to Ar}
\label{sec:period3-global-diis}

The first multi-$\ell$ implementation reproduced the second period but exposed a two-cycle SCF instability in aluminium.  The failure was numerical rather than element-specific: the direct and exchange fields alternated between two nearby density configurations while the total energy remained close to stationary.

The correction introduced here acts only on the fixed-point iteration.  For each angular/spin block we form the generalized commutator residual
\[
R = F P S - S P F,
\]
where $F$ is the raw Fock matrix, $P$ the occupied radial density matrix and $S$ the non-orthogonal Slater-basis overlap matrix.  A Pulay/DIIS extrapolation is then constructed from a finite history of $(F,R)$ pairs.  No atomic reference energies, TIR corrections or element-specific branches enter the iteration.

\subsection{Historical single-stage checkpoint}
A frozen v0.1 checkpoint used one parameter set for every neutral atom from sodium through argon,
\[
\text{basis}=15,\qquad N_r=600,\qquad \lambda_{\rm damp}=0.25,
\]
with DIIS beginning at iteration three.  In the environment in which the benchmark was produced, all eight systems converged and the recorded control energies were
\begin{align*}
E_{\rm Na}&=-161.8214667868\ E_h,\\
E_{\rm Mg}&=-199.5688114017\ E_h,\\
E_{\rm Al}&=-241.7481654795\ E_h,\\
E_{\rm Si}&=-288.6907148139\ E_h,\\
E_{\rm P} &=-340.6375397417\ E_h,\\
E_{\rm S} &=-397.2889482732\ E_h,\\
E_{\rm Cl}&=-459.2240885149\ E_h,\\
E_{\rm Ar}&=-526.6829149879\ E_h.
\end{align*}

The Hund-spin sequence of the $3p$ shell emerges from the configuration generator as
\[
\frac12,\ 1,\ \frac32,\ 1,\ \frac12,\ 0
\]
for Al through Ar.  The semantic-card layer stores this spin coordinate together with the outer-channel mean radius, radial width, energy and virial residual.  These are control observables only; TIR semantics and affective coordinates remain unassigned.

\subsection{Reproducibility correction: the robust ladder is normative}
A later full-suite CI run on Python 3.12.13 with newer NumPy/SciPy reproduced 101 tests but showed that the historical fixed single-stage parameters can leave aluminium just outside the convergence condition.  This does not license changing the aluminium physics or adding an element-specific damping constant.  Instead it confirms that a marginal numerical fixed-point recipe should not be the release contract.

The normative solver entry point therefore uses a global escalation ladder.  An atom advances to a larger basis and more conservative DIIS parameters only when either SCF convergence fails or the virial quality gate fails.  The escalation condition depends on numerical quality, never on the element identity.  The current regression test applies the same global ladder to every atom from Na through Ar.

This becomes essential in the $3d$ region, where false stationary points can satisfy an energy-only stopping rule while violating the virial check strongly.  A useful negative result is that a later numerical stage is not universally superior: if an earlier stage already satisfies the frozen physical quality gate, the robust algorithm accepts it rather than blindly maximizing basis size and iteration depth.

\paragraph{Current boundary.}
Na--Ar remains a tested control interval under the global non-element-specific quality ladder.  The historical fixed-stage energies remain a frozen benchmark rather than the cross-version convergence contract.  TIR remains absent from the fixed-point stabilization, and a numerical-library change that alters convergence must be recorded rather than hidden by loosening the scientific interpretation.
